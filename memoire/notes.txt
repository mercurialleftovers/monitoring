== vibration analysis notes

machine rotates => generates vibration => accelerometer captures said vibration, a voltage is generated correspondingly => things are plotted in time-waveform (amplitude vs time) and/or spectrum (amplitude vs frequency) via FFT.

Vibration is identified by the following components:

- Frequency – Used to determine how often a vibration is occurring
- Amplitude – Used to determine the severity/magnitude of a vibration
- Phase – Used to determine the *relationship between two signals of the same frequency*, measured in *degrees*

+ *Trend analysis*: is the practice of collecting historical data and examining it to spot patterns, directional movements, or recurring themes over time
+ *Time domain data*: time demain is the *amplitude variation* over *time*: a vibration signal is detected from a transducer (converts physical quantity into electrical signal), it is displayed on an oscilloscope as a waveform.

*Time Domain vs. Frequency Domain*: The time domain shows how a signal's amplitude changes moment-by-moment (like on an oscilloscope), whereas the frequency domain shows the strength (magnitude) and phase of each underlying sine wave frequency
